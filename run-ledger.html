The run

The packaging hierarchy

Each → case → layer → pallet → trailer, from the scenario's packaging profile. The pallet pattern is the ti-hi the optimiser computes: cases per layer in the better orientation, layers under the stack-height limit, reduced until the pallet's load limit holds.

Optimise the pallet pattern

Every candidate pallet for this profile's case, ranked by cases per pallet: the single-orientation grid, or a band layout when strips of mixed orientation pack more (the one-dimensional knapsack of Smith & De Cani, 1980). Then the what-if on this run's own volumes: how many inbound pallets and trailers the same eaches would need. Partial dispatch pallets do not change; racking, handling and stability for a different pallet type are not modelled.

Start to finish, per order type

The flow, as recorded

A layered Sankey over the recorded links: one link per unit that moved from one operation to the next, so returns split into restock and scrap and cross-dock bypasses storage. Columns are the longest path from goods-in; a bar's height is the larger of what entered and what left it. In units (retired only) every interior operation conserves; in eaches it does not, because picks leave stock behind.

What the planner asks

Work in progress over the run

What a handling unit costs

The spans between a unit's events, charged at the rates the run was recorded under (the planner's Analyze panel, defaults from analytics.js): a unit served at a station is charged that station's service time, whatever it waited; a moving unit is charged at the class of mover the floor contains. Cost per span = hours × labour rate (manned classes) + hours × capex ÷ amortisation years ÷ operating hours per year + hours × kW × energy price. Queue time costs nothing: holding cost is not modelled.
